1. One linear functional <w,x>_L on the hyperboloid = three neuron types, decided by the sign of <w,w>_L
====================================================================================================
(i)  time-like  : <p,x>_L = -cosh d(p,x)              max |error| over 2000 points = 4.26e-14
(ii) space-like : <w,x>_L = sinh(signed d(x, H_w))     max |error| over 300 points  = 2.07e-07  (distance found by brute-force search on the line)
(iii) light-like: -<w,x>_L = exp(Busemann_omega(x))    max |error| of the log      = 8.08e-14  (Busemann computed in the Poincare ball)
-> a ReLU(<w,x>_L + b) neuron is a ball-around-a-point detector, a geodesic half-space, or a horosphere detector,
   depending only on whether its weight vector is time-like, space-like or light-like. The model can learn which.

2. Yggdrasil attention (hyperbolic + torus phase + salience) as a single scaled dot product
====================================================================================================
float64: max |explicit pairwise - fused dot product| = 1.42e-14;  output stays on the hyperboloid: |<c,c>_L + 1/k| = 2.66e-15
float32: max |explicit pairwise - fused dot product| = 4.05e-06;  output stays on the hyperboloid: |<c,c>_L + 1/k| = 1.91e-06
squared-Lorentz logit  -(cosh d - 1)  vs  Gaussian-in-geodesic-distance logit  -d^2/2   for geodesic distance d = 0..6:
  -(cosh d - 1): [  -0.    -0.5   -2.8   -9.1  -26.3  -73.2 -200.7] 
  -d^2/2       : [ -0.   -0.5  -2.   -4.5  -8.  -12.5 -18. ]
-> same ordering of neighbours, but the Lorentz form falls off exponentially in d (a much sharper, 'only the closest matter' kernel).
