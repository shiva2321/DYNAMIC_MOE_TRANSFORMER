# FS-MoE growth: experts that grow as needed, a pool and a tier that grow, one governor (design, 2026-11-01)

The user asked (2026-10-31, again 2026-11-01): "experts can grow as needed and the system itself can be grown in both directions, horizontally and vertically, governed algorithmically, accurately and properly", and approved the plan "world that demands growth, notebook-first newborns, a governor, evaluation at equal memory". Same rules as before: dev seeds 700-702 for tuning, report seeds 710-714, predictions before any report-seed cell, nothing committed, growmoe/ and unimoe/ imported and never edited.

## What exists already (read from the code and notes)
* Within an expert: `widen`, `deepen`, `inherit` (function-preserving), and growmoe's `GrowthPolicy` (shadows grown at several scales plus an equally trained control, paired bootstrap on held-out losses, the cheapest candidate that passes, back-off after rejection); the gap rule of stage 4c (width from the within-batch fit gap) is held for the user's decision.
* The pool: the spawn rule with a probation buffer and an evidence test, infant protection; a wrong spawn is permanent (the merge routine undid 8 of 296); a full pool should share, not evict (merge research, 2026-10-16).
* A taller system: `growmoe/stack.py` (`StackMoE`, an `add_layer` growth step), never evaluated (R12b paused, D-10).
* Measured blocker (C-122, Q-41): growth almost never fires (3 of 70 runs; experts live a median of 5 batches, the rule needs 30), so nothing has shown whether growth helps.

## What is built (`fsmoe/grow/`, a compact lab; wiring into the production pool is a later step)
* `world.py`: a stream that demands growth. Inputs are directions on a sphere (d = 32); a regime is a patch around a centre (centres at least 0.3 apart in cosine) with its own labelling rule over 6 classes, from three families: `lin` (linear), `wide` (maximum of 24 linear pieces), `deep` (products of four tanh features). What a regime needs is set by an ORACLE, not assumed: three fixed networks (8 units, 64 units, 3 blocks of 8) are trained offline on 12,000 of the regime's own samples; `width` / `depth` when the wide / deep net beats the small one by 5 points (and the other), else `none`. Phases bring 4 regimes at a time; half of a phase's batches are old regimes. Scenarios: `mixed` (16 regimes: 4 lin, 8 wide, 4 deep), `easy` (8 lin: nothing needs growth), `big` (48 regimes: the flat gate becomes the cost).
* `system.py`: experts = closed-form notebook (unimoe `LdaHead`) + optional residual network (growmoe `MLPExpert`), answer = 50/50 mixture; routing by cosine to each expert's centroid with a per-expert acceptance cosine (a low quantile of its own, floored at 0.38); samples nobody accepts wait in a room; a dense group in the room founds an expert, or is ABSORBED by the expert it sits near (median cosine at least 0.25: its own outliers); test-then-train; held-out split per expert (15 %, never trained); multiply-add accounting (gate, expert, notebook per answer; training and growth trials in total).
* `governor.py`: one rule for four directions: grow only on evidence of need, check on held-out data that it helped, keep the cheapest option that works, undo if not, log why.
  1. **Notebook-first newborn, right-sized at birth.** A newborn is a notebook (answers from the first sample). When it has 300+ training samples a held-out test trains candidate networks (8x1, 8x3, 32x1, 64x1 units x blocks) and compares network + notebook with the notebook alone; the notebook stays unless a candidate wins by 2 points; otherwise the cheapest candidate within 1.5 points of the best is kept. A kept notebook is judged again when its data has doubled.
  2. **Wider / deeper.** Only an expert with a network that has trained 6+ batches, holds 600+ training and 100+ held-out samples and has held-out error above 0.04 is asked; the question is growmoe's `GrowthPolicy` (widen x2, x4, deepen; 240 steps); a commit is guarded for 2 looks and undone if the held-out loss is worse by more than 5 %; growth trials may cost at most `trial_ratio` (1.0) times the training so far; an optional parameter budget refuses growth.
  3. **More experts**: the room-and-absorb rule above (share, never found a duplicate).
  4. **A taller system (a tier).** When the flat gate (experts x 32 multiply-adds per sample) exceeds the budget of 640, experts are grouped by spherical k-means over their centroids (sqrt(E) groups) and routed in two steps (best groups, then experts in them); the tier stays only if it agrees with the flat routing on at least 98 % of held-out samples and costs at most 80 % of it; rebuilt when the pool has grown by 25 %.
* Arms: fixed experts F8, F16, F32, F64 (units x 1 block) and F8d3 (8 units x 3 blocks), no growth; **G** (notebook-first + governor, all four directions); Gng (no width/depth growth); GnoNB (network at birth + governor); Gnt (no tier); **BF** (brute force: every expert wider or deeper at every look, no test, no undo, capped at 128 units and 4 blocks).

## Measures
Mean and minimum test accuracy over regimes; parameters (network, notebook, total) and multiply-adds per answered sample (accuracy against memory and against compute); training and growth-trial multiply-adds; experts against regimes, purity (share of an expert's samples from its main regime) and the share of regimes with an expert of their own; growth against the oracle (share of width-need regimes served by an expert that is wider than the starting 8 units or has a larger candidate network, same for depth, and the false-growth rate on `none` regimes); tier: gate multiply-adds and agreement with the flat routing.

## Dev tuning (seeds 700-702, scenario `mixed`; report seeds untouched)
Knobs tried on G: promotion steps (300 / 200 / 150), trial ratio (2.0 / 1.0), candidate sizes (4 / 3). Mean accuracy over the three seeds: 0.733 (300 steps, ratio 2), 0.725 (150 steps), 0.717 (3 sizes), **0.733 (200 steps, ratio 1.0)** = the default G, chosen as the cheapest that matched the best. Other fixed choices (acceptance floor 0.38, absorb cosine 0.25, error trigger 0.04) were set while the lab was built on seed 700 and are not tuned further. Three design repairs came from dev runs, before any report seed: the room had to extract dense groups (a global tightness test never fired on mixed rooms), a group near an existing expert had to be absorbed by its median cosine (duplicates were founded from outlier tails), and regimes had to be kept 0.3 apart in cosine (two close regimes merged into one expert; this is the known near-twin problem and is kept out of this lab on purpose).

## What this lab does not show
It isolates growth decisions from recognition: regimes are well separated, routing is by centroid cosine, labels come with every sample. It is not the production pool (no conformal gate, no vMF signatures), and wiring the governor into `PhyloMoE` is a separate step. The oracle's `depth` class is small. Compute: governing (trials and promotion tests) costs several times the training in this lab; it is measured and reported, and `trial_ratio` limits the growth part.

## Dev findings that fix the predictions below (seed 700-702 only)
Mixed, mean accuracy over dev seeds 700-702: G 0.733 (about 56k parameters); on seed 700: F8 0.686 (34k), F16 0.702 (42k), F32 0.719 (59k), F64 0.723 (92k), F8d3 0.681 (54k), BF 0.727 (563k). Easy, seed 700: G 0.896 (14.6k, no growth event), F8 0.865 (17.1k), F32 0.864, BF 0.853. Big (48 regimes, first version without clusters), seed 700: G 0.800, F8 0.761, the tier was refused every time; an offline probe of the router showed why: two-step routing agreed with the flat routing on only 0.87-0.97 of samples at 55-80 % of its cost (also after adding a full-scan fallback for samples nobody in the best groups accepts), because regimes that are 0.3 apart in cosine form only weak groups. Governing costs much more than training (trial and promotion multiply-adds about 9 times the training on mixed, seed 700).

## Predictions (written before any report-seed run; scored by `fsmoe/scorecard_g.py` on seeds 710-714, means over seeds)
Equal memory in P2 means: among the fixed arms F8, F16, F32, F64, F8d3 those with total parameters at most 1.2 times G's.
* P1 (mixed) G's mean accuracy beats F8 and F16 by at least 0.02 each and is at most 0.01 below the best fixed arm.
* P2 (mixed) G's mean accuracy is at least that of the best fixed arm of equal memory.
* P3 (mixed) BF has at least 5 times G's total parameters and a mean accuracy at most 0.01 above G's.
* P4 (mixed) at least 80 % of the width-need regimes are served by an expert with at least 16 units in G.
* P5 (mixed) at least 40 % of the depth-need regimes are served by an expert with at least 2 blocks in G.
* P6 (mixed) at most 20 % of the none-need regimes are served by an expert with at least 16 units or 2 blocks in G.
* P7 (easy) G has at most 1 width/depth growth event per seed on average and a mean accuracy at least 0.01 above F8's with fewer total parameters.
* P8 (mixed) growth adds at most 0.02 to the accuracy of the notebook-first arm without it: G - Gng <= 0.02.
* P9 (mixed) G is at least as accurate as GnoNB (network at birth plus the same governor) minus 0.005, with no more total parameters.
* P10 (mixed) growth trials and promotion tests in G cost at most 12 times the training multiply-adds.
* P11 (big) G's mean accuracy is at least F8's + 0.02 with no more multiply-adds per answer.
* P12 (big) the tier is refused in at least 4 of 5 seeds (agreement under 0.98 or cost over 80 % of the flat gate).
* P13 (mixed, big) G ends with between 0.95 and 1.10 experts per regime and a purity of at least 0.95.
* P14 (mixed) G's worst-regime accuracy is at least F8's.
