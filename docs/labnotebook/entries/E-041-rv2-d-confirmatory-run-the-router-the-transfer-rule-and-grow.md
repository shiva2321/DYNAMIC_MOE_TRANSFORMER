## Question

## Why we asked it
The dev review (E-039) and the pre-registration (E-040). The user's standing rule: a repair becomes the default only after it passes its pre-registered test.

## Prediction (written BEFORE the numbers, if there was one)
All nine statements pass (E-040). Most likely to fail, in my order: S4-5, S4-8, S4-6, S4-7. Spec predictions: S4-1 +12 to +13 points, S4-2 +8 to +11, S4-3 0.0 to +2, S4-4 +8 with forgetting about 7 and 6 experts, S4-6 growth to 64-128 units, S4-8 46 to 48 % with forgetting about 7.

## Method (how)
704 cells of `cells_confirm.json` (ten stage-3 streams x 15 seeds x 4 arms, arithmetic operator-by-operator 6 seeds, banking77 5 seeds, arithmetic mixed from 16 and 128 units 15 seeds), run by `jobqueue.py run --phase confirm` from 2026-10-21 00:44 to 04:18 (EDT, 3 h 34 min; the machine shows 10:14 to 13:48 IST). The constants are the frozen ones (`grow_gap` 0.17, `transfer_margin` 0.25, router `free` with margin 0.01); the function-regime streams used the pool seed stream seed + 7. During the run I changed only the queue driver (not stamped): 12 threads instead of 9 at below-normal priority, and the arithmetic cells accounted as one core each (they still set three torch threads, so their numbers are those of the dev cells), the other cells got one or two threads from OMP_NUM_THREADS, where the dev cells took all cores (this can change floating-point rounding, not the algorithm). A power cut did not occur during the run (the PC restarted afterwards).

## Interpretation (what we understood, and on what basis)
Plain words. The three repairs work as the dev review said. The router fixes the pool's largest loss (choosing the wrong expert when tasks differ in one coordinate or overlap heavily) and does nothing where the old gate was already right. The transfer rule makes the pool found the right number of experts where the old pool founded too few; that is where its gain comes from, and it costs a few tenths of a point where the old pool was already right. Growth from the fit gap is a safety net: it stays silent on every stream except the one built to starve an expert, where it grows the 16-unit expert to the width of the 128-unit one.

Measured: all statements above on seeds used once. Not shown: that growth improves accuracy (its mixed-16 gain has an interval that includes 0), that the transfer rule's arithmetic gain is at least +5 with 95 % confidence (the interval's lower bound is +4.3; the pre-registered bar is on the mean), and anything about streams or sizes that were not run.

## Caveats and what went wrong
- S4-5 passes by a narrow margin on accuracy (-0.43 against the bar -0.5) and only with amendment A4-1 on the expert count (see above).
- The dev result for the transfer rule on arithmetic (+8.2, two seeds) was higher than the confirmatory one (+6.1, six seeds): the bar (+5) was met on the mean, not on the lower bound.
- The growth rule's -2.3 [-4.8, +0.2] on operator-by-operator arithmetic is not gated (the statements cover the mixed streams and the stage-3 streams) and is not significant with six seeds, but it is the one place where an adopted component lowers a measured number.
- Thread configuration: the arithmetic cells ran exactly as in dev (three torch threads); the function-regime and banking cells ran with one or two threads where the dev cells took all cores. This is a change of floating-point rounding only; I did not test whether any single cell differs.
- The audit script has the same author as the run. The independent reviewer's audit is open.
