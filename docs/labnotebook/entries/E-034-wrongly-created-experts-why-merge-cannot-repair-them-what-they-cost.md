## Question
The merge audit (E-033, F-28, Q-19) showed that the pool's merge routine removes no wrongly created expert. What does a wrong expert cost, what could repair it or contain the damage, which of those options work in a controlled test, and what must the user decide?

## Why we asked it
The user: a wrongly created expert is permanent, "this could make or break the entire system and may kill it silently". Follows E-033 (0 of 4 forced duplicates merged) and E-030 (the extend-or-spawn probe is close to a coin flip).

## Prediction (written BEFORE the numbers, if there was one)
None written down; this was exploratory. The first end-to-end study implicitly expected a large accuracy loss from duplicates and was wrong (F-29, F-30).

## Method (how)
- Constructed pairs (`study_lib.py`, `run_study.py`): 7 kinds of pairs x 6 seeds; candidate tests T0 shipped, T1 honest clone, T2 agreement on stored prototypes, T5 directional transfer with a margin sweep.
- Real experts (`arith_check.py`): the saved 6-expert arithmetic pool, 6 duplicates (12 directions) and 30 ordered pairs of different operators.
- End to end (`healing2_study.py`): 12 function regimes (4 classes, 32 inputs, experts of 32 hidden units, batches of 64), the stage-3 pool with the stage-4b transfer test on, pool seed = stream seed + 7. False spawns injected as clones with the extend option off: one per regime at batch 3 (configs n_a, n_a15, n_a20 with regimes of 10, 15, 20 batches), two per regime (n_b), one at batch 12 of 20 (n_m12). Arms: shipped, prequential references, label-free agreement at 0.8 and 0.5, rival tracking, held-out samples (1 in 8 withheld), consolidation variants, answer-time averaging; controls idshift, clean_h, none_h, clean_ens. Cap runs: drop, extend, evict (most redundant / least recently used), smart. Infant window: `infant_study.py`. 5 seeds (0-4) per cell, none of them a pre-registered test seed.
- Diagnoses: `healing_diag.py` (retracted version), `healing2_diag.py`, `twin_diag.py`, `idshift_check.py`, `alias_check.py`, `alias_check_s3.py` (see E-035).
- Literature: 19 sources searched 2026-10-16; four read in the text (Hughes and Sudderth 2013, Wang, Crammer and Vucetic 2012, DynMoE, MC-SMoE).

## Results (what came out)
Full tables: `research/growmoe_results/merge_study/RESULTS_MERGE_STUDY.md`, `RESULTS_HEALING2.md`, `RESULTS_INFANT.md` and the note. Headline numbers (all generated by `build_report.py`):
- The shipped merge merged 8 times against 296 injected false spawns in 20 runs; 0 of 42 constructed pairs (true duplicates included); T5 (directional, paired, one-sided) is the only candidate test that separates the pair kinds; on real experts duplicates have an upper bound of the excess loss of 0.04-0.38 nats, different operators 4.23 and more.
- A false spawn costs 3.7 points (one per regime), 4.3 (two per regime), 4.0 (15 batches), 4.3 (20 batches), 2.7 (false spawn at batch 12 of 20) against the same pool without false spawns; the false spawn answers about two thirds of the questions.
- Repair: rival tracking (live traffic, nothing withheld) removed 2.0 of 12 at 10 batches, 11.2 at 15, 11.2 at 20; held-out samples 10.6 of 12 at 10 batches but a net -3.7 points at two per regime (withholding costs 4.4 points there, 0.3 on a clean pool); label-free agreement removed 11.8 of 12 at threshold 0.5 and 0.0 at 0.8; 0 of 968 merges joined experts of different regimes.
- Answer-time averaging of overlapping experts: +1.5 / +3.7 / +0.6 / -0.1 points (configs a, b, a20, m12), difference 0.00 points on a pool without twins.
- Full pool (limit 16 / 20 / 8): drop 50.7 / 48.3 / 53.4 %, evict-else-share 66.1 / 64.6 / 59.0 %, unlimited reference 67.4 %; the regimes met after the pool fills are answered at 25 % (chance 25 %) under the silent drop.
- Infant window (no false spawn at all): regimes of 3 batches got their own expert in 1, 11 and 8 of 12 cases (3 seeds); with the protection switched off the pool founded 121, 121 and 69 experts for 12 regimes of 10 batches.

## Interpretation (what we understood, and on what basis)
Plain words. A wrongly created expert stays for ever, because the only code that can remove one tests experts against samples they memorised. It costs about 4 accuracy points and a slot; it does not hurt more when there are more of them, but the slots run out, and when they do the pool silently stops learning new regimes. Repair is possible when the survivor can be scored on samples that nobody trained on, and the cheapest source of such samples is the live stream (score the rival before anyone trains on the batch), which needs regimes of about 15 batches or more. When several experts share a regime, removing twins does not return the accuracy, answering with their average does. A full pool should evict a redundant expert if there is one and otherwise share the nearest. Technical statement: measured on one synthetic family (function regimes, clones as false spawns), 5 seeds; not measured: real streams with natural false spawns, dormant duplicates, compute and memory, B3 (provisional spawn).

## Caveats and what went wrong
- The first healing study (F-29) injected at batches 6 and 9 of 10; the newborn infants accepted the next regime's inputs, so natural spawns fell to 1 of 12 and the reported -20 points were a blind pool. Retracted; kept in `healing.json`, `healing_diag_s0.json`.
- The second version (F-30, E-035) compared against a clean pool that had a head start from seed aliasing; it made false spawns look 2-3 times as costly. Its files (`healing2_[a,b,a20,cap*,m*].json`, `RESULTS_INFANT_offset0.md`) are kept; the corrected ones carry the prefix `n_` and the id-shift control now equals the clean pool.
- Heredocs in this environment collapse backslash escapes and corrupted two of my edit scripts; both were caught by syntax checks. Rule unchanged: edit files that contain backslashes with the Edit or Write tool.
- Regimes of the bench are far apart on the sphere, so wrong merges across regimes are unlikely by construction; the pair study covers harder pairs ("partial20": a loose margin retires the expert that knows more, -13.6 points).
