# E-017 - Routing v2 spikes: a fingerprint shortlist, what overlap does, and the batch vote (with the failures)

- **ID:** E-017
- **Date:** 2026-10-10
- **Track:** G
- **Type:** experiment
- **Status:** completed
- **Evidence grade:** B (dev seeds 100-102, patched copies of `PhyloMoE.observe` made at run time, nothing pre-registered)
- **Code / commit:** throwaway scripts in `research/growmoe_results/rv2/` (commit `f881cb3`); no product file touched at the time
- **People:** Claude; user: "can a unique fingerprint or wave or hypervector of an expert's knowledge, with resonance, cut the gate scan?" and "let's address 1 (cheaper gate) and 3 (overlapping, messier data) first"

## Question
(1) Can a cheap per-expert fingerprint replace or shorten the exact gate scan, which is 97 % of the compute? (3) Why does accuracy fall when regimes overlap in input space, and what fixes it?

## Why we asked it
E-015 left two gaps: the pool is not cheaper than a single network, and the repaired pool was only tested on well-separated regimes.

## Prediction
None; direction-finding. (Pre-registered tests follow in E-019 and the Stage-2 PREREG.)

## Method (how)
Eight throwaway spikes on a trained pool: fingerprint candidates in front of the exact gate (centroid, k-means sub-centroids, random Fourier "wave" features, ridge routers, strided prototype landmarks) scored by shortlist recall, accuracy and multiply-adds; a controlled-overlap stream (10 regimes, centre cosine rho = 0 / 0.3 / 0.6 / 0.85, a different hidden function per regime); onset diagnostics (what the gate does when a regime starts); a label-aware assignment; the batch vote; its safety on mixed batches, on the standard streams, and on known regimes later arriving 70/30. All tables are generated by `report_spikes.py` into `RESULTS_RV2_SPIKES.md`.

## Results (what came out)
- **Cheaper gate.** One centroid or 4-8 k-means sub-centroids per expert as stage 1, then the exact conformal gate on the top-2 experts: F-15 46.1 % vs exact 46.0 % at 6.8x fewer multiply-adds (k = 4); F-30 45.3 vs 45.2 at 12.0x; S2-30 88.4 vs 88.6 at 8.5x. The saving grows with the number of experts (by formula, not measured, about 170x at 1,000). S2-5 and S1 have 3-4 experts: nothing to save; a centroid alone fails on S1 (64.7 %).
- **Overlap collapses accuracy.** Pool 50.5 / 33.7 / 18.4 / 16.8 % at rho = 0 / 0.3 / 0.6 / 0.85 while oracle-routed fresh experts stay at 48 %; the regimes remain separable from x alone (nearest centroid 100 / 100 / 99.9 / 90.1 %); test-time routing is within 3 points of its own bound, so it is not the bottleneck.
- **Cause at rho = 0.3: training contamination.** Only 78.4 % of what an expert trains on comes from its own regime. The **batch vote** (all accepted samples of a batch train the expert most of them were routed to, if it holds >= 50 %) gives purity 99.5 % and accuracy 47.2 % (oracle 48.0). Safe on F-15 (46.0 -> 50.4), S1 (78.2 -> 79.1), S2-5 (identical), 50/50 mixed batches (30.7 -> 36.2); on known regimes that later arrive 70/30 the plain vote costs 1.3 points on the minority at rho = 0 (47.7 -> 46.4) and gains 11.1 at rho = 0.3.
- **Cause at rho >= 0.6: regimes merge** (3.3 experts for 10 regimes at 0.6). The per-sample novelty test rejects 88.7 % of foreign inputs at 0.6 and only 0.8 % at 0.85, while a **batch-level fingerprint test** (distance of a 64-sample mean from the expert's prototype mean, bootstrap 99.9 % threshold) detects 100 % of foreign batches with 0 % false alarms in isolation.
- **What did not work.** A Fisher combination of the per-sample novelty p-values inside the live pool **over-fired** (72 / 131 / 89 experts for 10 regimes at 0.3 / 0.6 / 0.85): its null was not calibrated. Random Fourier "wave" features and ridge routers on them were worse than a plain centroid (wave-512 on S2-30: 76.0 % alone). Strided prototype landmarks were worse than k-means sub-centroids at equal cost (16 landmarks: 87.8 % at 6.1x vs 8 sub-centroids 88.5 % at 7.5x). Label-aware assignment added only +1.6 / +2.8 at rho = 0.3 and +0.5 at 0.6. Restricting the vote to samples the majority expert's gate accepts was 1-3 points weaker on single-source overlap and cost 0.3 point on the minority.

## Interpretation
The user's "fingerprint / resonance" idea is right in a specific form: a **cheap first pass that shortlists** experts, with the exact test kept for the decision; and, applied to a **set** of inputs rather than one, it is far more powerful than per-sample tests (this is why the batch is the unit that matters). The dominant accuracy loss under moderate overlap is contamination during training, not routing. The naive way to bring set-level evidence into the live pool does not work without a properly calibrated null.

## Caveats and what went wrong
Dev seeds, patched copies, a handful of variants tried per question (a forking-paths risk): everything here is a direction to be tested on fresh seeds. My first safety hypothesis (the restricted vote protects minorities) was not supported; the plain vote was kept as the primary and the restricted one as a fallback.

## Consequence
Design (E-018): stage 1 batch vote, stage 2 fingerprint shortlist with an exact "second look", stage 3 a calibrated batch-novelty monitor behind its own spike.
