# Prior art: the closest published work to our combination

**First pass, 2026-11-02 (M0 step 6).** About 16 web searches and 6 page fetches, read at abstract or search-summary level; nothing here was read in full. Source tags: **A** = abstract page opened, **S** = search summary only. This is a check for what we may honestly claim, not a literature review. A miss is possible; M8 repeats the check with a fuller search before anything is called new.

**The combination we want to describe:** a task-free pool of small experts on a frozen or slowly changing representation, where (1) a *calibrated* test (conformal p-values with Benjamini-Hochberg control) decides when something is new, (2) every expert has a closed-form head that cannot forget, (3) experts have a lifecycle (dormant, merged, removed), (4) knowledge can be removed exactly, and (5) the cost is measured on one GPU against compute-matched baselines.

## What this says

1. **Task-free expansion by a novelty signal is an established line** (CN-DPM 2020, VariGrow 2022, DEM 2023, SEMA 2025, LargeMonitor 2026). Collecting what the experts reject in a buffer and then training a new expert is the CN-DPM pattern; our probation buffer is a relative of it, not a discovery.
2. **Closed-form heads are established, and so is exact removal by ridge downdating** (the analytic line; ACU 2025; the 2026 federated paper). For us exact removal is a capability we need (F6), not a novelty we can claim. What may still be ours is doing it per expert inside a pool, where isolation avoids the coupling that a single shared head has.
3. **Not found in this pass:** (a) a calibrated, false-discovery-controlled test that decides when to add an expert in a task-free stream (the search said so explicitly); (b) closed-form heads inside an expanding pool with a lifecycle of dormant, merged and removed experts; (c) a compute-matched, single-GPU cost accounting of such a pool. Roughly 16 searches at abstract level do not prove absence.
4. **What we may honestly write in a report:** that we combine known ingredients, name each one with its source, test the combination under pre-registered, compute-matched evaluation, and report where it ties the strong simple baselines. We do not write "first", "novel" or "new method" until the M8 check supports it.
